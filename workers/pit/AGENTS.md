# Pit

## Teams, archives, Overview, Monitor, batteries

- **Pit** (`workers/pit`): every table carries `team_id` and `settings` is keyed by team and name (migration `0011`). Settings are the team's `eventKey`, `nexusEventKey` and `iframeUrl`; the team number is the request's team's (`teamNumberOf`). The Blue Alliance and Nexus API keys are always the worker's `TBA_AUTH_KEY` / `NEXUS_API_KEY` secrets, for every team, and never a setting. `test/teams.test.ts` has its settings and isolation tests. **Checklist archives** (`src/archives.ts`, migration `0012`, table `checklist_archives`): the Checklists page's "Archive and reset" (`POST /archives { event, type, details }`, anyone signed in) saves every list, item and issue as one JSON snapshot with who archived it and when, and unchecks every item in the same batch; open issues are left alone. `type` is `match`, `practice` or `other`; the event is the team's `eventKey`, else its `nexusEventKey`, whatever the page sent, and only free text when it has neither. `GET /archives/defaults` gives the pop-up that event, and `GET /archives/next-match` the team's next official match there (The Blue Alliance's `next_match_key`, else Nexus's first match that isn't on the field; "Qual 42", "Playoff 5", "Final 2"); since it calls those services, the pop-up asks for it only when it opens and the Overview every two minutes. **Overview** (`apps/pit/src/plugins/overview/`, Pit's first page, at `/`): four tiles, each a figure that opens its page: checklist items checked, open issues (and how many are new since the last archive), the Next Up battery with the In Robot one under it, and the next match (away from an event, the last archive). **In the pit now** (`apps/pit/src/shared/in-the-pit.tsx`, on the Overview as a fifth, full-width tile that opens Schedule, and as a section on the Monitor): who has a role of type `pit` on the team's Schedule at this moment and until when, from Schedule's `GET /now?type=pit` through `apiPath("schedule")`, every minute; hidden while the team has Schedule off (`useAppOn`/`appOn`). Each figure is asked for on its own (`GET /lists`, `/issues`, `/batteries`, `/archives`, `/archives/next-match`) and keeps what it last had, so with no connection the page shows what it last knew and never an error. **Resolving an issue keeps its row** (`checklist_issues.resolved_at`; the open-issue routes leave it out) until the next archive has recorded it, and that archive deletes it in its batch, so an issue reported and resolved between two archives is still in the second, once. An issue's status in a snapshot: `resolved` (resolved since the archive before), else `open` (it was open in the archive before's snapshot) or `new`. An issue goes with its item when the item is deleted. Any new read of open issues needs `isNull(checklistIssues.resolvedAt)`. The Archives page (`/archives`, `apps/pit/src/plugins/archives/`, `GET /archives` a page at a time, `GET /archives/:id` with the snapshot) shows each archive as a panel that opens to every list in one list. Pit's Logs page (`/logs`, `plugins/logs/`) is for the robot's match logs, which the pit log downloader will fill (roadmap L.2); for now it's empty. A list's own Reset and `POST /reset` archive nothing. `test/archives.test.ts`. **The Monitor's match list** (`MatchesSection` in `plugins/pit-monitor/pit-monitor-page.tsx`) is all of the team's matches, played ones with their scores and a W, L or T in place of the times: `GET /monitor/data` adds a `result` (`{ red, blue, winner }`, null until played) to each match from The Blue Alliance's `/event/:key/matches` (`src/monitor-matches.ts`: Nexus's matches matched by name, "Qualification 12" is qm12, and checked by their teams). On a wide screen the Monitor's grid ends at the bottom of the window (`useFitToWindow`), so the match list and the checklists shrink and scroll inside their columns, with the table's header and the overall progress bar kept at the top; the list scrolls to the next match. **Our next match** (`plugins/pit-monitor/our-next-match.tsx`) leads the Monitor: `GET /monitor/data` adds `ourMatches` (`ourSchedule` in `src/monitor-matches.ts`, types exported as `@g3/worker-pit/monitor-matches`; null with no schedule): the team's next match, its alliance, both alliances' teams by driver station (`redTeams`, `blueTeams`), Nexus's status, `matchesUntilQueuing` (before the call: the matches until it, from the last match Nexus has past Queuing soon), `matchesUntilField` and the queue, on-field and start times. With Nexus, a match of ours before the last one On field is over even before its score is in; on The Blue Alliance's schedule (no statuses) the event is at the match after the last score. A Nexus match with no times takes The Blue Alliance's (`timesFromTba`). It's two boxes of the same height under its own heading. The match box is a bar of our alliance's color (a pale tint and a bright 3px border, heavier than the page's own) with the match and its Queue and On field countdowns in our alliance's text color, and the alliances under it like upside-down browser tabs, the whole one rectangle: ours merges into the bar (with a rounded inside corner, `TabCorner`), and the other sits behind the bar's edge and our half's middle border, showing its border only on its bottom and outside edges; blue always left and red right, all six teams by station and ours ringed in white, each with its rank (The Blue Alliance's rankings) and pit address (Nexus's `/event/:key/pits`) beside it when known and when its half has room (`ranks`, `pits` on `ourMatches.next`). The status box beside it is the urgency's color (surface, then green, amber, red) with the matches until we're called (or Nexus's status), a fixed 17rem that fits the widest ("queuing"), so the match box doesn't change width between states. Up next (`NexusSection`) is the event's On deck, Now queuing and Queuing soon, laid out like the battery tiles, with real Nexus data only. Neither shows start times. A played qualification match's score, three digits wide so they line up, has The Blue Alliance's bonus-RP dots beside it (`result.bonus`, from `score_breakdown` by the season's fields in `BONUS_RP`; the event snapshot keeps them too). **Battery timers** (`src/battery-timers.ts`, migration `0018`): a battery's time in its state counts only while the team's timers run: `counted_ms` up to `counting_since`, null while paused. The Batteries page's Pause All / Unpause All (`POST /batteries/timers { paused }`, anyone signed in) stops or restarts every timer and changes no battery's state; a state change starts the timer at zero, paused while the team's timers are. On a published Schedule event's days, the first day's opening resets every timer to zero and pauses it (someone unpauses once the chargers have power), each day's closing pauses and a later day's opening carries on. Pit reads the days from Schedule's `GET /internal/teams/:id/event-days` over its `SCHEDULE` service binding (at most every 2 minutes, none while Schedule is off), and `catchUp` applies each change at the moment it was due, from the next request that reads or changes the batteries, so nothing runs on a timer. `GET /batteries/timers` gives `{ paused, next }`. `test/battery-timers.test.ts`. **Event snapshot, dev and staging only** (`src/event-snapshot.ts`, `snapshotAvailable`: `isStaging` or a `LOCAL_GATEWAY_URL`; never production): Pit's Admin page's Use event snapshot (`GET`/`PUT /admin/event-snapshot`, admins) makes `GET /monitor/data` answer from `src/snapshots/2026gagr.json` (GRITS 2026 as Qualification 31 went on the field: the schedule, the first 30 results, Nexus-style statuses and rankings worked out from those results, taken from The Blue Alliance's public pages) instead of The Blue Alliance and Nexus, with a Snapshot badge on the Monitor. Its times are offsets from that moment, replayed from when it was switched on (the internal setting `eventSnapshotSince`). Its clock (now − that setting) decides each match's Nexus status (`statusesAt`, in order like Nexus: the last match due on the field, 5 minutes before its start, and every one before it are On field, the next is On deck and the one after Now queuing, those two only within 30 minutes of their start so a break waits) and whether its score is in (5 minutes after its start); rankings stay as they were, and In the pit now shows eight of the demo's students (`sampleInThePit`, shifts ending in GRITS's time zone) in place of Schedule's. Admins move the clock from the Monitor (`SnapshotClock`, `plugins/pit-monitor/snapshot-clock.tsx`: ±1 match, ±5 and ±1 minutes, Reset) through `PUT /admin/event-snapshot/clock { minutes | matches: 1 | -1 | reset: true }` (`movedClock`, kept within an hour of the first and last matches), which moves the setting. `test/event-snapshot.test.ts`. Decided: a team's edit to a shared catalog part makes that team's own copy; each app keeps team settings in a team-keyed table in its own D1; apps send Slack messages with the team's own installation (from G3ID).

## AdvantageScope

- **AdvantageScope Lite at Pit's `/scope/`** (roadmap L.5): Mechanical Advantage's own browser build, from `vendor/advantagescope` (a shallow git submodule pinned to a stable release, v26.0.2; never an alpha or beta, which expire) with `apps/pit/advantagescope/gearbox.patch`. **It looks exactly as upstream ships it** (decided 17 October; `docs/roadmap/ui-alignment.md`): no shared parts, no team colors. The patch only changes where it reads and saves: `src/main/lite/teamStorage.ts` stands in for `localStorage`, the log and asset paths go to `/api/scope/...`, `?log=<event>_<name>.wpilog` opens a match log, and the 3D view's field and robot workers get their models from the page (`src/shared/renderers/field3d/workers/gearboxFiles.ts`: the page fetches them and passes the bytes, since Firefox sends a worker's own requests past Pit's service worker, so offline they failed). Keep it that small; upgrading is moving the submodule to the next stable tag and fixing the patch.
- **Building** (`apps/pit/advantagescope/build.mjs`, `pnpm --filter @g3/pit scope:build`, also the first step of Pit's `build`): copies the release into `apps/pit/advantagescope/.build`, applies the patch, builds it as upstream builds Lite (`npm ci --ignore-scripts`, `ASCOPE_DISTRIBUTION=LITE`, the field and robot assets from GitHub, the docs) and writes `apps/pit/public/scope/` (gitignored), with `bundledAssets/index.json`. Needs network and **Emscripten 4.0.12** (`emcc`); it skips everything when `public/scope` was built from the same release, patch and script, and `ADVANTAGESCOPE=skip` leaves it out. `deploy.yml` caches the build and installs Emscripten only on a miss. `pnpm dev` doesn't build it: run `scope:build` once for `/scope/` in dev (Vite serves `public/scope`, with `/scope/` as its `index.html`). Pit's offline service worker doesn't precache `/scope/` (too big for every Pit user); `apps/pit/public/scope-cache.js` keeps it as it's used (below).
- **The worker** (`workers/pit/src/advantagescope.ts`, at `/api/scope`, answering what AdvantageScope's Python server answers on a robot): `GET /logs` (the team's stored match logs, whatever folder it asks for) and `GET /logs/:file` (gzipped, the browser unzips it); `GET`/`PUT /settings` (**the team's**, not one browser's or person's: each localStorage key it writes is a row in `scope_settings`, migration `0016`; anyone signed in saves, the last save wins); `GET /assets` (the team's uploads in AdvantageScope's index form, with each `config.json`), `GET /assets/:name/:file` (R2, with ETags), `POST /assets` (Upload Asset: a zip of one asset or of several, mentors and admins, read by `src/unzip.ts`; at most 50 MB a zip, 10 assets and **100 MB a team** in all (G3's 2026 robot alone is 62 MB), counted unzipped (`unzip.ts` stops a file at the size its zip states). To stay in a Worker's memory the zip is held once, as the request's body (`multipart.ts`, not `formData()`), every file is unzipped and checked once before anything is stored, then each is streamed into R2 (`FixedLengthStream`), never held whole; one with the same name replaces it) and `DELETE /assets/:name`; `GET /asset-list` for the Logs page; `GET /sign-in` (a signed-out visitor goes to the team's ID sign-in and back, only to a `/scope/` page). Asset files are in the `LOGS` bucket under `pit/teams/<team>/scope-assets/`, rows in `scope_assets`. Both tables are in the tenancy lint, the delete and export hooks and the isolation test; the demo blocks saving settings and uploading. `test/advantagescope.test.ts`.
- **Models download small and once** (cellular at events): `.glb` files are gzipped, since Cloudflare doesn't compress `model/gltf-binary`. `build.mjs` writes each built-in model's `.glb.gz` beside it, and the worker sends it for `/scope/bundledAssets/*.glb` (`bundledAsset`, through `run_worker_first` and the `ASSETS` binding; it falls back to the `.glb`); an uploaded `.glb` is stored gzipped in R2 (`putGzipped`, in 8 MiB multipart parts, `contentEncoding: "gzip"`; older uploads are plain) and sent with `Content-Encoding: gzip`. Both asset indexes give each file other than `config.json` a version (a hash of a built-in file, `<uploadedAt>-<size>` for an upload), and `apps/pit/public/scope-cache.js` (imported into Pit's service worker) keeps each file in Cache Storage under that version, downloading it again only when the index has another, and drops what the index no longer lists. Match logs too, once opened: the service worker asks `GET /logs/:file` with `X-Gearbox-Raw: gzip` (sent as stored, `application/gzip`), keeps it gzipped in its `scope-logs` cache and unzips it for AdvantageScope (`DecompressionStream`); a kept log is checked by its ETag (`"log-<id>"`, 304 while it's the same row) and used as it is when Pit doesn't answer in 4 seconds. Pit's AdvantageScope page reads that cache (`useSavedLogs`, `shared/saved-logs.ts`, again on the worker's `scope-log-saved` message): its log picker says "On this device" or the download's size, and logs Pit no longer has are let go. The Logs page shows how many are on the device, badges each one, and has Save all to this device, shown only while the service worker runs the page (`canSaveLogs`): first `saveAdvantageScope` (every file in `/scope/files.json`, which `build.mjs` writes, plus the settings, the log list, the team's uploads and the built-in FRC fields and robots, each fetched through the service worker, which keeps it), then each log missing (`saveLog`: the request carries `X-Gearbox-Save`, which the service worker keeps without unzipping and answers 204). AdvantageScope itself opens offline once it's been opened online: everything under `/scope/` but `bundledAssets`, and `GET /api/scope/settings` and `/api/scope/logs`, come from the network with a copy kept in the `scope-app` cache by path (rewritten only when the ETag changed; Cloudflare redirects `www/hub.html` to `www/hub`: a fetch that followed it is kept as the page itself, and a page load, which the browser follows itself, is kept as `www/hub`, which offline answers `www/hub.html` too; a kept copy is answered as a new `Response` with no address (`asAnswer`): in Firefox a page answered with one carrying another address isn't run by the service worker, so its workers' models went to the network), used without a connection. Pit's AdvantageScope page asks for `navigator.storage.persist()`. Asset paths can't carry a version: AdvantageScope finds `model_0.glb` by cutting `.glb` off the path.
- **Connecting live doesn't work**: an https page can't open AdvantageScope's plain `ws://` connection to the robot. That stays in the Driver Station's or the desktop app's AdvantageScope.
- **Pit's AdvantageScope page** (`/logs/advantagescope?log=<file>`, `plugins/logs/advantagescope-page.tsx`; under Logs, which stays the bar's current page, with no bar entry of its own): the team's match logs to choose from, and `/scope/` in a frame filling the rest of the window; choosing a log loads it again with `?log=`. Full screen (the browser's, and Pit's `useFullscreen`, which hides the bar) leaves only a small bar with the log picker and Exit full screen; Esc or leaving the page ends it. Both modes keep the same elements, so switching never loads AdvantageScope again (the header is written out in `Page`'s look for that reason). The Logs page's Open AdvantageScope and each log's Open in AdvantageScope go there. The Logs page also lists the team's AdvantageScope assets (mentors remove them; `plugins/logs/scope-assets.tsx`).
