# @g3/ui: shared bar, theme and team appearance

## Editable team appearance

- Identity admins edit appearance on the Appearance page of the team's admin pages (Portal's `/admin/appearance`, `apps/portal/src/plugins/admin/appearance-page.tsx`; G3ID's old `/admin/team-ui` redirects there). A team that hasn't saved any gets `teamUiDefaults(team, name)`: the site team's from site.ts, any other team its registered name, its number as the short name ("9999 SHOP") and its FRC links; the editor's Reset uses the same (`GET /admin/team/ui` returns `defaults`). `TeamUiSettings` and defaults live in `packages/site-config/src/team-ui.ts`; the team-keyed D1 record is read by public `/team/ui` and edited through admin-only `/admin/team/ui`. **The team's name is the one saved here:** a save that changes it renames the team in G3ID's `teams` and in the platform's registry (`renameTeam` in `workers/g3id/src/lib/team-ui.ts` → the platform's `PUT /internal/teams/:id/name`, best effort), so the console, ID's sign-in page and anything that asks for the team's name say the same (migration `0018` caught up G3ID's copy once).
- Shared UI loads colors, logo, display font and team names at runtime (`packages/ui/src/team-ui.ts`), and **every app's colors come from them**: nothing in an app is a fixed brand color or a fixed gray. `applySettings` sets the variables `colors.css` declares (`--g3-<name>` for the showing theme, `--g3-light-<name>` and `--g3-dark-<name>` for both, `--g3-brand`), the primary palette, the neutral ramps and the browser's `theme-color`. How each kind of app picks them up:
  - **Tailwind apps:** `primary-*` for the team's color, `bg-surface` for cards (not `bg-white`), `bg-page`, `bg-inset`, `border-line`, and the `secondary-*` (or `gray-*`) ramp for grays. In light mode the ramps are built in and stay exactly so on the default colors; a team that changes its light card, inset, border, text or muted color gets ramps built from them (`applyNeutrals`: 100 inset, 200 border, 500 muted, 900 text, mixes between). In dark mode `theme-palette.css` builds the ramps from the dark colors the same way.
  - **The top bar** (`nav-bar.css`): surface, border, muted text for links, text for the current page.
  - **Scouting** (plain CSS, `apps/scouting/src/styles.css`): every brand color and gray is an expression over the `--g3-light-*` / `--g3-dark-*` variables that gives the old color on the defaults (a rule under `html[data-theme="dark"]` uses the dark ones, any other rule the light ones). Write new rules the same way.
  - **Attendance** is a Tailwind app; its QR code displays use the `--g3-*` variables and pin the dark theme (`forceTheme("dark")`), so they show the team's dark colors.
  - **What stays fixed:** colors that mean something (green for done or signing in, red for an error, deleting or signing out, amber for a warning, status badges, the red and blue alliances, chart ramps, other companies' logos). The platform's public pages belong to no team and keep the built-in colors.
- **A team's color is its accent, one per theme** (`light.accent`, `dark.accent`); there is no separate primary color. The light accent is the brand color (`brandColor(settings)` in `@g3/site-config`): the primary palette (`--color-primary-50…900`, built in `packages/ui/src/team-ui.ts`) is built from it, and the app icons take it in both themes. In dark mode the palette's tints (50–300) are dark (the brand color sunk into the page), and its solid shades (500 and darker: buttons, selected tabs, focus rings) and brand text (`text-primary-*`) take the dark accent, which `packages/ui/src/theme.css` builds from `--g3-dark-accent`. So a theme's buttons are filled with that theme's accent (`fillColor(settings, mode)`). A team that keeps the default light accent gets `builtInBrandColor` (`#a32035`): the hand-tuned palette in `index.css` and the icons as drawn.
- The editor's Colors section has the two accent boxes, each with saturation and brightness sliders (HSV; helpers in `packages/site-config/src/color.ts`) and a strip showing the accent on that theme's backgrounds. With **Link the light and dark accents** on (`linkAccents`, the default; only the editor reads it), a color entered in either box gives the other its hue, and the other keeps its own saturation and brightness (a gray or black has no hue to give); off, both are free. The sliders only ever change their own box, never its hue. The other palette colors are under "More options", closed by default (a field in it that can't be saved opens it), where each theme has a preview (`ThemePreview`): a small mock page using all seven of its colors, with the button in that theme's accent and button text.
- **Button text color** (`buttonText: { light, dark }`, each `auto`, `light` or `dark`; `auto` by default): the text on buttons and every other fill in the team's color, chosen for each theme on its own. `buttonTextColor(settings, mode)` in `@g3/site-config` gives the color: Light is the dark palette's text color, Dark the light palette's, and Automatic whichever of the two has more contrast (`contrastRatio`, WCAG) against that theme's accent, which fills its buttons (`fillColor`). The editor has it under each accent as three joined buttons, with an "On a button" swatch beside "On the page" and "On cards". `@g3/ui` sets it as `--g3-on-brand` (the showing theme's; `--g3-light-on-brand` and `--g3-dark-on-brand` for both), which Tailwind apps use as `text-on-primary` (`stroke-on-primary` for a check mark). **Every fill in the team's color takes its text from it**: `bg-primary-500` and darker with `text-on-primary`, Scouting's `var(--g3-brand)` backgrounds with `color: var(--g3-on-brand)`. `text-white` is for fills that aren't the team's (green, red, a status color). The knob of a switch stays white. The platform's own pages belong to no team and keep white. A record saved before the choice existed reads as `auto`.
- Records saved with the old `primaryColor` are read as current ones (`withoutPrimaryColor` in `workers/g3id/src/lib/team-ui.ts`): a brand color the team chose becomes its light accent, and a dark accent it never chose takes that hue. Public `/team/ui` still sends `primaryColor` (the brand color) for pages loaded before the change, which build their colors from it; `PUT` refuses it.
- App icons follow the team's brand color (`packages/ui/src/team-icon.ts`). Each icon file is a black tile with a white drawing and one accent in the built-in brand color (`#a32035`); for a team with another brand color, the SVG is read in the browser and that accent swapped. `useTeamIcon(src)` / `<TeamIcon>` from `@g3/ui` do it for an icon on a page (the top bar's, the portal's tiles), and `applyTabIcons` (run with the rest of the appearance) for the page's own `<link>` icons: the tab's SVG, and its `.ico` fallback and home-screen icon redrawn as PNGs. The files don't change, a team with the default color gets them as they are, and the platform's own public page keeps the default. **A new app icon must use exactly that accent hex** to be recolored.
- Portal resource links (public site, Slack, Discord, GitHub, Instagram, FRC-Events, The Blue Alliance, Statbotics, match13, FRCTools Orders and FRCBOM; the last two default to the same address for every team, `toolLinks` in `team-links.ts`) have editable HTTPS URLs and `hiddenLinks` controls. Hiding preserves the URL; removing clears it. Empty or hidden links are omitted, including the public-site link on ID's sign-in page.
- **Apps grid order** (Team Appearance, `appOrder`): every Portal tile's key (`PortalTileKey`: the team's apps in `portalAppLabels`, and its links), once each, in the team's order; admins drag them on the editor (`apps/portal/src/plugins/admin/app-order.tsx`, `@dnd-kit`). `completeAppOrder` fills in tiles a saved order doesn't name (one added later) in their default places, both when G3ID reads a stored record and on Portal; a stored record without one gets `defaultAppOrder`. G3ID rejects an order with a tile missing, repeated or unknown. Hidden or empty links keep their place but don't show. A new Portal tile needs a key in `portalAppLabels` or `teamUiLinkLabels` and a place in `defaultAppOrder`.
- **The team's logo is its avatar** (`useAvatar`, off by default): the 40 × 40 px image a team makes each season for event screens, from The Blue Alliance at `teamAvatarUrl(number)` (`@g3/site-config`: `https://www.thebluealliance.com/avatar/<current calendar year>/frc<number>.png`). Off, or while it hasn't loaded, pages show the app icons. `usePageTeamAvatar()` / `useTeamAvatar(number)` from `@g3/ui` give its address only once it has loaded (`teamHasAvatar`, checked once per page), so a team without one never shows a broken image: the top bar, Portal's public-site tile, Scouting's team logo and the pit log downloader (online only) use them. The Appearance page's Use Team Avatar switch is off and disabled, with the reason, for a team that hasn't submitted an avatar to FIRST (The Blue Alliance serves them). There is no logo address any more: a record saved with a `logoUrl` reads as `useAvatar` on when it had one.
- Reset to defaults restores the editor form; Save applies it. Stored settings from older versions gain new link and setting defaults without resetting their branding or restoring explicitly empty links. Current writes require the full schema.

## Shared navbar and light/dark mode

- Every app's top bar is `AppNavBar` from `@g3/ui` (`packages/ui/src/components/app-nav-bar.tsx`, styled by its own plain CSS in `nav-bar.css` so it renders the same in Scouting, which doesn't use the shared Tailwind theme). Each app's `shared/nav-bar.tsx` is a thin wrapper: it filters pages by permission, marks the current one (`activePath`: the most specific link covering the URL), and passes react-router links via `linkWith(Link)`. The menu button (top left) is there at every width and opens a drawer: under the heading "All Apps", the team's home ("Portal") and then its apps that are on, in the order of the home's grid, with the one you're in marked. Below 768px the pages move into the drawer too, above the apps and a dividing line; above it they stay across the bar, and the drawer is only the apps. There is no separate All Apps link.
- **Who's signed in and which apps are on** come from `packages/ui/src/session.ts`, asked once per page load and shared: `useSignedInUser()` (G3ID's `/auth/me` through `apiPath("id")`) and `useTeamApps()` (the platform's `/team/apps`; "all" when it can't say). The bar greets the user ("Hello, <name>!", a link to the Account page on Portal) at its right end at every width, on a phone too, where the bar's other controls are in the drawer; a name that doesn't fit is cut short with "…". Never for a kiosk PIN session. `AppNavBar`'s props: `allApps={false}` for no app list (Shop's kiosk mode), `account={false}` for no greeting (the same), `team={false}` for the platform's own pages, which belong to no team (the operators' console: built-in colors and icon, no team name, app list or greeting, nothing asked of ID), and `signedIn` for an app that signs people in and out without reloading (ID passes what it knows, so the bar follows). An app passes `actions` only for controls of its own (Portal's Sign in, Shop's kiosk badge).
- Light/dark is one setting for all apps: the `g3_theme` cookie on `.g3robotics.com` (shared across ports on localhost), applied as `html[data-theme]` before first paint by a small script in each app's `index.html`; `useTheme`/`setTheme` (`packages/ui/src/theme.ts`) change it, and open tabs pick up a change on focus. No cookie → the system setting.
- One color scheme for every app (`packages/ui/src/colors.css`): page `#f4f6f7` / dark `#171717`, surface (cards, top bar) `#ffffff` / `#262626`, inset `#eef1f3` / `#1e1e1e`, line, text, muted and accent, as `--g3-*` CSS variables that switch with the theme. Those are the defaults; a team's own colors (Team Appearance) replace them at runtime. Tailwind apps use them as `bg-page`, `bg-surface`, `bg-inset`, `border-line`; every page's root background is `bg-page` and every card's `bg-surface`. `--g3-brand` is the team's color for solid fills (the showing theme's accent, so the same as `--g3-accent`) and `--g3-on-brand` the text on them (the team's button text color for the showing theme); `--g3-light-*` and `--g3-dark-*` are both themes' colors whichever is showing, for a rule written for one theme.
- Apps are styled light. Dark mode comes from `packages/ui/src/theme.css` + `theme-palette.css`: neutral ramps (secondary, gray, slate, ...) invert onto the dark palette's colors, tinted backgrounds (`*-50..300`) become dark tints, and dark text shades (`text-*-600..950`) become light. Solid fills (`bg-*-500/600`) are unchanged, except the team's own (`bg-primary-500` and darker), which take the dark accent. So write light-mode classes; use a hex value (`bg-[#24292f]`) only for a color that must not change in dark mode, and never for the team's color or a gray.

## Tutorial mode

**Tutorial mode** (computers only, a window at least 1200px wide): the menu's **Tutorial mode** (beside Need help?) shows the same docs page in a panel on the right third of the window, in the docs' compact view (`?embed` in `apps/platform/src/docs/docs-app.tsx`), and the two follow each other: a page the reader opens in the panel sends the app to the page that doc is about (the first path in its `pages` without a `:name` part; an app's `index.md`, its home), in the same app or another one (the docs page's `postMessage`, `TutorialDocMessage`; `followDoc` in `AppNavBar`). So **put a doc's main page first in its `pages`**. In the panel only, the docs' previous and next links skip the sections of apps the team has off and the docs about pages the reader can't open: each doc's `access` (`everyone`, the default; `mentor`: mentors and admins; `admin`), set from who the app's page is for (the app tells the panel the team's apps and the reader's role, `TutorialAppsMessage`). **Keep a doc's `access` in step with who can open its page.** It's one setting for every app (the `g3_tutorial` cookie; `useTutorial`/`setTutorial`, `packages/ui/src/tutorial.ts`), never on a screen with no app list (a kiosk). While it's open, `<html>` has `data-tutorial` (`nav-bar.css`): the body is narrowed and transformed, so `position: fixed` fills the app's part, and `#root` scrolls instead of the window. So **pages never call `window.scrollTo`, read `window.scrollY` or set `document.body.style.overflow`**: use `scrollPageToTop()`, `pageScroller()` and `lockPageScroll()` from `@g3/ui`.
